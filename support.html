Opening books

How do I open a book?

Any of these:

  • Double-click an EPUB in Finder (once Plainread is your default app for it, see below).
  • Drag the book onto Plainread's window or its Dock icon.
  • File › Open… (⌘O).
  • File › Recently Read for the last 10 books.

Each book opens in its own window. Plainread reads the book where it is: nothing is copied, imported or moved.

How do I make Plainread open EPUB books when I double-click them?

Apple Books stays your default EPUB app until you choose otherwise:

  1. In Finder, select any .epub book and choose File › Get Info (⌘I).
  2. Under Open with, choose Plainread.
  3. Click Change All…, then Continue.
Which books can Plainread open?

EPUB 2 and EPUB 3 books: ordinary .epub files, and the unzipped EPUB folders that Apple Books keeps in iCloud and in its backups. Books whose fonts are scrambled to protect them (a common publisher practice) open normally.

"This book is protected by DRM"

Some stores encrypt their books so they can only be read in the store's own app: books bought in Apple Books, Kobo, Google Play Books and others. Plainread can't open those, and won't try to remove the protection. Books from publishers that sell without DRM, from libraries of public-domain books, and EPUBs you made yourself all work.

Can I add notes or highlights?

Not yet. Plainread is a reader and never changes your books. To keep a passage, use Copy with Citation (⇧⌘C) and paste it into your notes.

Reading

How do I change the typeface, size, width or theme?

Everything is in the View menu, and in Plainread › Settings… (⌘,):

  • Font: System Serif, System Sans, Georgia, Palatino or Charter.
  • Appearance: follow your Mac, or always light, sepia or dark.
  • Zoom In, Zoom Out and Actual Size (⌘+, ⌘−, ⌘0).
  • Wider and Narrower (⌘], ⌘[) set the line width.
  • Justify Text (⌘J).

Your choices apply to every book and are remembered next time. Plainread keeps your place in the book when you change them.

What does "Publisher Styles" do?

By default Plainread sets the book in your chosen typeface and line spacing, while keeping the book's own italics, small capitals, centred lines and indents. View › Publisher Styles (⇧⌘Y) shows the book in the publisher's own fonts and spacing instead. The colours always follow your theme, so a book stays readable in dark mode.

How do I use the table of contents?

The table of contents sits beside the book. Click a chapter to go there; the chapter you're reading is marked as you scroll. Show or hide it with View › Show Table of Contents (⌘T) or the button beside the window's close, minimise and zoom buttons. At its foot you'll see the printed page (when the book records one) and how far through the book you are.

Go › Next Chapter and Previous Chapter (⌥⌘→, ⌥⌘←) move a chapter at a time.

Does Plainread remember where I was?

Yes. Each book opens where you left it, even after you move or rename the file, as long as it's the same book.

How do I search a book?

Edit › Find… (⌘F) searches the whole book, not just the chapter on screen. ⌘G and ⇧⌘G move between matches, and Esc closes the find bar.

What happens when I click a link?

Links within the book, such as notes, cross-references and the book's own contents page, jump to the right place. Web and email links open in your browser or mail app. A book can never load anything from the internet by itself.

Copy with Citation

How does Copy with Citation work?

Select a passage and choose Edit › Copy with Citation (⇧⌘C). Plainread copies the passage in quotation marks, followed by the author's surname, the year and the printed page:

“I lived there two years and two months.” (Thoreau 1854, 1)

A passage that runs across a page break gets a page range. Quotation marks inside the passage become single ones.

Why is there no page number?

An EPUB has no fixed pages: the text reflows to fit the window. Many publishers record where each printed page begins, and Plainread uses those markers. When a book doesn't include them, the citation has the author and year only.

The year is wrong

Plainread uses the date in the book's own information, which is sometimes the date of the e-book edition rather than of the original publication. Check it against the book's copyright page before you cite.

Can I use a different citation style?

Plainread › Settings… offers Chicago author-date (Scott 1998, 42), APA (Scott, 1998, p. 42) and MLA (Scott 42).

Quick Look & Finder icons

How do I preview a book in Finder?

Select a book in Finder and press Space. Plainread's Quick Look extension shows the cover, title, author and contents, followed by the book's text in Plainread's style. Very long books show their first part; open them in Plainread to read the rest.

Space shows a generic icon, or books have no covers in Finder
  1. Make sure Plainread is in your Applications folder, and open it once. macOS only finds its extensions after the app has been opened.
  2. Check the extensions are turned on. On macOS 15 or later: System Settings › General › Login Items & Extensions, then click the info button next to Quick Look and turn on Plainread. On macOS 14: System Settings › Privacy & Security › Extensions › Quick Look.
  3. If another app also previews EPUB books, macOS uses only one of them. Turn the other one off in the same place.

Spotlight

How does searching inside my books work?

Spotlight already keeps track of every file on your Mac, but on its own it can't read inside an EPUB. Plainread includes a small Spotlight importer that reads each book's title, author, publisher and text for Spotlight. Then a search in Spotlight (⌘Space) or in a Finder window finds a phrase in any book, wherever you keep it.

The importer runs inside Spotlight, on your Mac. Plainread itself never looks through your folders, and nothing is sent anywhere.

Spotlight doesn't find words in my books
  1. Make sure Plainread is in your Applications folder, and open it once.
  2. Log out and back in (or restart) once after installing Plainread. Spotlight looks for new importers when you log in.
  3. Give Spotlight time. It reads books in the background, and a large library can take a while the first time.
  4. Folders listed in System Settings › Spotlight › Search Privacy are never searched.
  5. Books with DRM can be found by title and author, but their text is encrypted, so Spotlight can't search inside them.
